How modern LLMs are actually built.

A hands-on reading course on the internals of large language models — from tensor shapes to trillion-parameter training runs. Each page goes deep on one topic, with runnable PyTorch cells (real torch, via a local kernel) and exercises you can check off. Covers the full stack: architecture, training science, distributed systems, data, post-training, and case studies of frontier open models.

How to use this

Read in order, or jump to what interests you — the start here pages are the foundations everything else builds on. Every topic is a full deep dive: edit the code cells and hit ⌘/Ctrl + Enter to run, and check off exercises as you go. Your edits and progress persist locally in this browser. Each page ends with a self-assessment checklist.

The cells run real PyTorch on a small local kernel. Start it once from the project root — python3 kernel/server.py — and the status pill in the bottom-right corner turns green. Until then, cells stay editable but won't execute.

Topics

The idea behind the pages

Working on LLMs draws on four kinds of fluency: (1) practical ML coding — writing and debugging real model and training code; (2) ML & systems depth — architecture, training dynamics, distributed systems, numerics; (3) experiment design — "loss spiked / eval regressed, what now?"; and (4) reading the field — knowing what frontier labs actually do and why. The pages are organized around exactly these four, with runnable code wherever a claim can be demonstrated rather than asserted.

Content is written from first principles and cross-checked against primary sources (papers, framework docs). Where a claim is subtle, the page links the source.
