Case Study: Inkling — and What Everyone Agrees On

Thinking Machines' first open-weights model (July 2026) makes deliberately contrarian architecture bets — no RoPE, mostly-local attention, encoder-free multimodality. The second half of this page zooms out: across DeepSeek-V4, Qwen 3.6, the Kimi line, Inkling, MiniMax and GLM, the same handful of ideas keep winning. That convergence list is the fastest way to get oriented in the current landscape.

1. Inkling in numbers

975B total / 41B active MoE, 1M context, multimodal in (text, image, audio) / text out. Pretrained on 45T tokens of text + images + audio + video. Muon for the large matrices, Adam for the rest (Optimizer Frontier §4 — now simply the standard recipe), on GB300 NVL72 systems.

The contrarian architecture bets

  • Mostly-local attention: 66 layers — 55 with a local 512-token window, 11 global (a 5:1 local:global ratio). Same hybrid logic as Qwen's 3:1 DeltaNet mix, but with windowing as the cheap layer instead of a recurrent state.
  • No RoPE at all: a learned, input-dependent relative-position bias — and in the global layers the bias only covers the preceding 1,024 tokens, beyond which attention is effectively position-agnostic. A bet that long-range order matters less than content once you're far away.
  • Kernel-4 convolutions after the K/V projections and on attention/MLP outputs — cheap local token mixing that softens the burden on attention itself (an echo of the short-conv components in the DeltaNet family, Attention Variants §4).
  • Encoder-free multimodality: no separate pretrained vision/audio encoder — raw-input patches feed the one transformer, trained from scratch. Contrast with Kimi K2.5's MoonViT encoder + DEP machinery: Inkling deletes the component instead of load-balancing it.
  • Small touches: RMSNorm immediately after the token embedding; controllable "thinking effort" exposed as a first-class inference knob.

The post-training story

Bootstrap SFT on synthetic data — including data generated by Kimi K2.5 — then spend most of the compute on asynchronous RL scaled past 30M rollouts. Two things to notice: an American frontier lab openly bootstrapping from a Chinese open-weights model's outputs (the OSS flywheel is real, and it's the distillation economy in action), and async RL as the main course, not the garnish — echoing Inkling's siblings (MiniMax's Forge RL over 200k+ real environments; Kimi's agentic RL).

3. The distilled takeaways

        
    
          
  1. Full attention is now a garnish. Every flagship makes most layers cheap
            (linear state, compression, or windows) and keeps a minority of full-attention layers for
            recall. The design question has moved from "which attention?" to "what ratio, and which
            cheap mechanism?"
    2. 
          
  2. Muon won the optimizer transition — but only with a stability guard
            (QK-clip, or careful AdamW-exception lists). Nobody runs it naked at scale.
    3. 
          
  3. Sparsity is the scaling axis. Total:active ratios have gone from ~4:1
            (Mixtral) to ~30:1 (V4-Pro) to ~50:1-class (K3). "How big is it?" is now two numbers.
    4. 
          
  4. Stability is engineered in, not tuned in. Doubly-stochastic residual
            mixing, weight-level logit clipping, hash-routed early layers — constraints that make
            explosions impossible, replacing the spike-triage playbook
            (Training Dynamics §3).
    5. 
          
  5. Post-training is where products differentiate. Specialist→OPD merging
            (V4), agent-swarm RL and token-budget RL (Kimi), 30M-rollout async RL (Inkling),
            200k-environment RL (MiniMax). Pre-training sets the ceiling; RL and distillation decide
            what ships — and increasingly, what it costs per answer.
    6. 
          
  6. Data mixture beats data volume — again. K2.5's early-fusion-at-10–20%
            result and Inkling's 45T multimodal-from-scratch both land on: mix modalities in early and
            lightly, don't bolt them on heavily at the end.
    7. 
          
  7. The OSS ecosystem is a flywheel. Inkling bootstraps from K2.5 outputs;
            GLM-5 adopts DeepSeek's sparse attention; everyone publishes the tricks. Reading these
            reports is the state of the art — there is no secret canon anymore.

Why this matters

          

            "What's interesting in recent open models?" is a near-certain conversation. A senior answer
            names the convergences (hybrid attention, Muon+guard, extreme sparsity, RL-heavy
            post-training) and then one sharp divergence to show real reading — e.g. Inkling
            deleting the vision encoder where K2.5 built DEP to load-balance one, or V4 compressing the
            KV stream where Qwen replaced it with a recurrent state. Trends show awareness;
            disagreements show understanding.
